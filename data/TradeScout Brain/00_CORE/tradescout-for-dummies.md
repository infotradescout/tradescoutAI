# TradeScout for Dummies

TradeScout is your local participation operating system. It brings together people, services, money, and community tools in one place so you can get real-world things done without juggling a bunch of disconnected apps.

At a high level, TradeScout helps three big groups of people:

1. **People getting projects done**  
   Homeowners, renters, and property managers who need to plan and complete local work: repairs, improvements, maintenance, and one-off jobs.

2. **People offering services**  
   Contractors, pros, and local businesses who want to show up in the right local moments, respond quickly, and build trust over time.

3. **People organizing spaces and groups**  
   HOA boards, community organizers, neighborhood leaders, and anyone coordinating shared spaces, rules, budgets, or events.

## What Scout actually does for you

Scout is the local search and summary surface that runs TradeScout. Instead of clicking around blindly, you can tell Scout what you are trying to do and let it orchestrate the right tools, pages, and actions.

Here are some examples of what Scout can handle:

- Turn "I need a new roof soon" into a plan: rough timeline, cost ranges, and questions to ask.
- Help you compare local pros, not just find them.
- Keep track of projects, notes, and decisions over time so you don’t lose the thread.
- For pros, suggest ways to improve your profile, follow up on leads, and show up in the right local searches.
- For communities and HOAs, help you organize boards, documents, and recurring work.

You should think of Scout as the front door: tell it what you are here to do, and let it route you to the right place.

## How TradeScout is different from a marketplace

TradeScout is not just a list of contractors or a feed of random posts. It is designed as an operating system for participation:

- **Verified activity over vanity reviews** – the focus is on real jobs, interactions, and context, not just star ratings in a vacuum.
- **Local first** – everything is anchored in counties, communities, and local networks so results feel relevant where you live or work.
- **Multi-role by design** – the same person can be a homeowner, a contractor, and a community organizer; TradeScout is built to handle that.

## First wins by role

When you are just getting started, here are the simplest high-value first wins:

- **Homeowners / residents**  
  - Post your first project or maintenance need so Scout surfaces a scope for it.  
  - Save a couple of promising local pros so you have a short list ready.

- **Contractors / pros / businesses**  
  - Claim or create your profile and fill in the basics (service area, trades, contact options).  
  - Turn on lead notifications for the types of work you actually want.

- **HOA / property / community organizers**  
  - Create or link your community space.  
  - Invite a few key people (co-board members, trusted neighbors) so decisions and history live in one place.

## How roles and experience adapt over time

During signup you can select all the ways you plan to use TradeScout (getting projects done, offering services, organizing community, etc.), but that is just a starting signal.

Scout and the app will refine your experience based on what you actually do:

- The more you post projects or requests, the more the experience tilts toward helping you get things done.
- The more you respond to leads or update a profile, the more tools and analytics for pros surface by default.
- The more you manage groups, events, or shared spaces, the more the HOA/community tools come forward.

You can always tell Scout directly if your role changes (for example: "I also run a small painting business"), and it will adjust recommendations and shortcuts.

## How to get unstuck

If you are ever unsure what to do next, you can:

- Open the Help Center and use the quick actions or articles for your role.
- Search with Scout in plain language, for example:  
  - "Give me a tour of TradeScout for homeowners."  
  - "Show me how to get more local leads."  
  - "Explain TradeScout for Dummies and suggest my next step."
