## 1) One-sentence definition

TradeScout is: the local operating system for community interaction.

## 2) What TradeScout is not

TradeScout is not:
- a lead-selling marketplace
- a pay-to-play exposure engine
- an open-contact directory where visibility grants access

## 3) Mission

Mission:
Build the trusted county-by-county system that lets people discover and act on local opportunity without sacrificing consent, safety, or integrity.

What "connection without compromise" means in practice:
- Discovery is open enough to create awareness, but contact is never automatic.
- Every contact path is gated: Intent -> Decision Card -> Contact.
- Exposure is governed by trust and context, not by who spends the most.

## 4) Core doctrines / non-negotiables

List the rules that should never be violated.

- Visibility does not equal access.
- All contact must route through Intent -> Decision Card -> Contact.
- Claims-first signup with adaptive/contextual verification is mandatory.
- County is the operational container; intelligence must route into county_metrics, county_entities, county_notes.
- Trust/CVS governs exposure, and UI/admin cannot bypass precomputed intelligence or trust logic.

## 5) Who it serves

Primary users:
- local real estate operators (investors, agents, wholesalers, acquisition teams)
- trusted local service participants who need governed introductions (contractors, lenders, title/closing, specialists)

Secondary users:
- county ecosystem participants who need read-only visibility and context
- team leads/ops managers who need workflow governance, compliance, and measurable trust signals

Who should get value first:
- active county operators with immediate deal flow pressure who need better signal and safer contact paths now

## 6) Scout's role

Scout is:
the only bridge between discovery and action; an intent interpreter and policy-safe execution layer.

Scout is not:
an unrestricted messenger, a bypass around trust gates, or a replacement for county intelligence precompute.

Scout must always:
- gather and interpret intent before action
- assemble county context and trust context before connection
- enforce platform law and route outcomes through approved gating states

Scout must never:
- grant direct contact because someone is visible
- bypass Trust/CVS or decision-card requirements
- invent intelligence on the fly that conflicts with stored county snapshots

## 7) Discovery, trust, and action

How discovery should work:
Users can discover people, opportunities, and county-level signals with enough depth to decide whether to proceed, without automatic access to private channels.

How trust should work:
Trust is a system signal, not a vibe. It is built from behavior, verification quality, consistency, and contextual relevance, then converted into exposure permissions via CVS thresholds.

How action should work:
Action begins only after intent is declared and evaluated. The decision card captures why a connection is requested, who is affected, and whether trust/context requirements are met before contact is allowed.

What "Intent -> Decision Card -> Contact" means operationally:
Intent creates a structured request.
Decision Card computes and records eligibility, context, and risk.
Contact is granted only if the decision passes trust policy for that county/context.

## 8) County-first model

Why county-first matters:
Real estate action is local, regulation/context varies locally, and trust quality collapses when systems ignore county reality. County-first keeps intelligence actionable and accountable.

What county intelligence includes:
- county_metrics (facts, quantified conditions, trend snapshots)
- county_entities (who/what is assigned or active in that county)
- county_notes (human context, constraints, nuances)

What should be precomputed vs generated on the fly:
Precompute: intelligence snapshots, trust-relevant county signals, entity assignments, and routing outcomes used by gating.
On-the-fly: presentation formatting, user-specific summaries, and Scout narrative framing that does not alter policy-critical facts.

How county-by-county network effects should work:
Each county should become more useful as verified participation grows, signals get sharper, and successful governed connections increase confidence, which attracts better participants and raises signal quality again.

## 9) Trust / CVS

What CVS should represent:
CVS is the system's confidence-weighted trust and suitability signal for exposure decisions in a given context.

What affects it:
- verification depth and reliability
- behavioral consistency and policy compliance over time
- contextual match quality (intent, county, role fit, outcome patterns)

What it should never become:
- a vanity score people can game for status
- a pay-to-inflate number tied to spend

How thresholds should work:
Thresholds should be policy-defined by action sensitivity and county context. Lower-risk actions can tolerate lower thresholds; higher-risk or higher-impact contact requires stronger CVS and verification evidence. Threshold logic must be auditable and stable.

## 10) Monetization

We charge for:
- software capability (workflow, automation, orchestration, team tooling)
- intelligence depth and operational analytics
- enterprise-grade governance and integrations

We never charge for:
- direct access to a person
- priority contact rights that bypass trust gates
- rank inflation tied to spend

We never want to become:
- a lead broker
- an ad auction for attention
- a platform where money substitutes for trust

Allowed monetization examples:
- per-seat/per-team subscriptions for advanced operating features
- tiered intelligence packages with more depth and tooling
- enterprise contracts for governance, controls, and integration support

Question marks / unresolved monetization ideas:
- usage-based pricing vs flat subscription for high-volume operator teams
- whether premium verification services should exist and how to avoid perceived pay-to-play
- how to package county expansion value without creating access distortion

## 11) GTM / rollout

Best first wedge:
One county, one high-urgency operator segment, one clear workflow outcome (better qualified connections with lower noise).

Best county type first:
Counties with active transaction velocity, fragmented coordination, and enough participant density to generate early trust network effects.

Why:
Fast feedback loops, measurable before/after outcomes, and enough surface area to prove that governed connection beats open-contact chaos.

What repeatable expansion looks like:
Prove one county playbook -> codify onboarding + trust calibration + ops metrics -> expand to adjacent counties with similar conditions -> refine per archetype (urban/suburban/rural) without breaking core law.

## 12) Product hierarchy

Load-bearing core:
- contact gating pipeline (Intent -> Decision Card -> Contact)
- county intelligence substrate (metrics/entities/notes + jobs)
- Trust/CVS exposure control

Core extenders:
- Scout orchestration and guided execution
- workflow automation and team operations layer
- analytics/feedback loops for trust and outcome quality

Satellites / later:
- broader community features beyond immediate action paths
- expanded ecosystem integrations
- advanced AI surfaces not required for core gating/trust outcomes

Things currently stealing time:
- non-core surface expansion before core metrics are fully hardened
- edge-case UX polish that does not improve trust/action throughput
- tooling tasks that should be automated once then removed from manual loops

## 13) Long-term picture

If TradeScout works, the world looks like:
Local opportunity networks become higher-trust, lower-noise, and faster to act in. People can be visible without being vulnerable. Quality participation compounds because policy and trust are predictable.

What becomes possible that is not possible now:
- county-level collaboration without uncontrolled inbound spam
- scalable introductions that preserve consent and context
- measurable trust governance that drives better outcomes over time

What standard TradeScout is trying to establish:
Permissioned connection infrastructure where discoverability, trust, and action are linked by enforceable policy rather than by money or attention hacking.
