# LISA Product Definition

## What LISA is
LISA is TradeScout's organized truth layer.

Its job is to:
- take verifiable TradeScout actions and observation signals
- normalize them into stable market-state facts
- interpret those facts in natural language
- keep stored conclusions current as reality changes

LISA is not a chatbot, not a UI widget, and not a generic analytics dashboard.

## What LISA is not
- not raw database access
- not direct user or household data export
- not freeform AI narration detached from evidence
- not a source of contact bypasses or gating bypasses
- not a replacement for Scout routing
- not a replacement for the human layer (judgment, contact gates, local work)

## Mission (how we talk about this — no product change required)

We are **not** selling “AI.” We are selling a **better way to operate**:

- **Creators and users stay in control** of decisions, publishing, and contact.
- **AI assists** — it expedites appropriate tasks (draft, search, filter, stamp,
  route). It does **not** replace decision-making, intent, or the human layer.
- **Assist, don’t replace.**
- **Reduce** storage churn, drift, and unnecessary compute — and still get
  **~10× better results** because work starts from what is current and real
  (OR / LISA), not from a blank prompt and a pile of stale generations.

Permission and on-device context exist only to support that mission: clearer
intent for *them*, less waste, same success path — never to harvest a corpus
or automate away judgment.

This messaging aligns with Operational Reality (Merlin
`OR_PARTNER_OUTLINE.md`) and does not require changing TradeScout gating,
Scout, CVS, or LISA behavior to be true.

## Selective inheritance (the model, not just a tool)

Selective inheritance is **how OR works**, not a one-off audit checklist.

Generic AI **regenerates**. OR **inherits selectively**:

| Inherit | Discard |
|---|---|
| What is still true (LISA current findings, 4data, verified outcomes) | Stale, superseded, redundant generations |
| Human intent, decisions, and creator control | Silent automation of judgment or contact |
| On-device context the user already has (with permission) | Copying their life into our data center “for the model” |
| Proven routes (Scout → Decision Card → Contact; county containers) | Parallel systems that bypass law |
| Prior art / keep lists that still earn their place (e.g. community makeover) | Dead duplicates, fake features, quarantined surfaces revived whole |

Rules of the model:
1. **Start from what already works and is still current** — do not blank-prompt the world.
2. **Keep the human layer** — inherit assistance paths; never inherit “replace the person.”
3. **Expire and supersede** — inheritance without freshness is drift (the ~20%).
4. **Refuse the ~80%** — if storage, bandwidth, or generation isn’t necessary for this
   outcome, don’t create it.
5. **Parity** — inheriting on-device / local-first must not weaken success.
6. **Name keep vs discard** — every change surface (product, copy, permissions, code)
   should be able to say what is carried forward and what is left behind.

Community makeover packets use selective inheritance as a **process tool**.
That tool is an instance of this **model**. Same model applies to LISA truth
maintenance, Scout routing, Continuum “edit once / source stays alive,” CVS
action reflection, and device-context permissions.

## AI-economy alignment (doctrine facts)

These are product facts, not vibes. They govern LISA, Scout, county
intelligence, community signals, CVS reflection, and any edge/phone
compute TradeScout adopts:

1. **The failure mode of big AI is replacing the human layer.**
   TradeScout systems assist, route, and explain. They do not substitute
   for human decision, Intent → Decision Card → Contact, or local
   accountability. LISA explains truth; humans act.

2. **Intent is one of the areas AI lacks the most.**
   Models guess wording; they do not own *why the person is here* or what
   they are willing to do next. TradeScout treats intent as a first-class
   scarce signal — captured, confirmed, and gated (Scout interpretation →
   Objectives / Decision Card → Contact) — never inferred silently into
   power. Freeform generation without durable intent is a primary source
   of the ~20% drift. Device context may *clarify* intent for the user;
   it must not invent intent for the system.

3. **~20% of generative responses are drift** — off-evidence, stale,
   overconfident, or brand/law divergent. Drift is treated as a defect.
   LISA’s job is freshness, provenance, and
   `current | stale | superseded | suppressed` reconciliation so drifted
   narration cannot remain “truth.”

4. **~80% of storage, bandwidth, replies, and generations are redundant,
   stale, or unnecessary.** The winning system is the one that **does not
   create that waste**: prefer live indexed signals, supersession over
   accumulation, retrieval of what is true now over regenerating what was
   already known, and on-device prep that never uploads noise. Phones /
   edge compute exist to cut the 80%, not to become another generation farm.

Implication for “pocket data center”: permissioned local sensing + light
on-device work that feeds LISA’s truth maintenance — not a distributed
excuse to generate more stale text into the cloud.

## User-owned device context (permission doctrine)

Every user already carries **gigs of personal context** and **hundreds of
gigs of device storage** — photos, files, location history, messages,
documents, drafts. That context exists **for their experience**, not as a
default TradeScout corpus.

Rules:
- TradeScout does **not** treat the phone as free training/storage inventory.
- Capabilities we already need (camera, files, precise location, local
  drafts, share sheets) are mostly **permission grants + clear purpose**,
  not new infrastructure.
- Every permission ask must state **exactly why this way**: what stays on
  device, what (if anything) is uploaded, what LISA/Scout/CVS will do with
  it, and how to revoke.
- Prefer **on-device use of their context** to improve *their* Scout,
  community, Home Vault, and proof flows. Only promote a signal into
  county/LISA truth when the user intentionally publishes or completes a
  gated action.
- File sharing, photo pickers, and share targets should lean on the OS the
  user already has — ask for access with purpose, don’t rebuild a parallel
  cloud drive “because AI.”
- **Parity rule:** data staying on their device must not reduce
  functionality, success rates, or outcomes. Local-first is architecture,
  not a degraded mode. Scout still routes, Decision Cards still work,
  Direct Connect still completes, community still posts — the difference is
  *where* context lives and *when* anything leaves the device, not whether
  the product works.

### How we ask (OR trust framing — anti-“AI wants your data”)

Aligned with Operational Reality (Merlin `OR_PARTNER_OUTLINE.md`), the
mission above, and **selective inheritance as the model**: we do not pitch
another AI product. We inherit what is already true and useful for *their*
outcome; we discard harvest theater, blank-prompt waste, and decision
replacement.

Do **not** frame permissions as “help our AI learn” / “improve the model” /
“personalize ads.” That triggers every reason people refuse AI.

Frame as **their control and their outcome**:

| Instead of | Say / do |
|---|---|
| We need access to your photos | To stamp this proof for *your* job / post — stays on your phone until you publish |
| Allow location for AI | So Local only shows what’s near *you* — we don’t invent your county |
| Upload your files to the cloud | Pick a file once; we use it for this request; nothing joins a training pile |
| Enable everything at signup | Ask **at the moment of need**, one capability, one sentence why |
| Trust our AI | You’re in control — we assist; you decide |

Hard presentation rules:
- Lead with **their success and control**, never “our AI.”
- Name the **non-goal** in the ask: “Not for training. Not for sale. Not replacing your decision.”
- Prefer OS pickers / share sheet (user chooses the artifact) over broad library access.
- Default deny; skipped permission still gets full product via manual entry / typed intent (parity).
- Never batch “AI permissions” into one scary wall.

This is how device context joins OR without looking extractive: the phone
supports assist-not-replace and cuts waste; TradeScout does not harvest a
life feed or automate away judgment.

This is how the 80% waste rule meets the product: don’t copy their life
into our data center; let their pocket make *their* session better, with
consent they can understand — and with the same success path either way.

## Core responsibility
LISA answers three questions:

1. What is true right now
- current market state
- current system state
- current county/category/surface motion

2. Why it is true
- source families
- evidence
- freshness
- confidence/provenance

3. What changed
- whether an older stored conclusion is still valid
- whether a finding is stale, superseded, or suppressed

## Truth maintenance rule
LISA must never allow stale or disproven findings to remain treated as current truth.

That means LISA is not just a feed generator. It is a reconciliation system.

When newer verified signals arrive, LISA must:
- re-evaluate existing findings
- mark stale findings as `stale` or `superseded`
- store the newer finding as the current truth
- preserve provenance so the update path is explainable

## Canonical inputs
LISA should consume normalized signal bundles, not random UI state.

Primary input families:
- `scout_interactions`
- `objectives`
- `observations`
- `home_scout_listing_events`
- `bot_ui_findings`
- persisted crawler/bot visibility telemetry
- trust/CVS-aware exposure facts
- market signal aggregates

## Canonical outputs
LISA outputs organized findings.

Each finding should answer:
- what happened
- why it matters
- how fresh it is
- where it applies
- whether it is still current

Minimum output shape:
- `id`
- `headline`
- `narrative`
- `sourceKind`
- `priority`
- `freshnessMinutes`
- `truthStatus`
- `scopeType`
- `scopeRef`
- `evidence`
- `engineVersion`

## Scope model
LISA findings should be scoped so they can be consumed safely.

Supported scopes:
- `global`
- `county`
- `category`
- `surface`
- `partner`

Examples:
- county truth: `Escambia County HVAC demand accelerated in the last 24 hours`
- category truth: `Luxury resale verification demand is rising`
- surface truth: `HomeScout Listings inventory pressure increased`
- partner truth: `Cumulus-eligible activation readiness is up in Mobile County`

## Stored findings model
LISA should persist findings, not just render them live.

Each stored finding should include:
- finding identity
- scope
- generated timestamp
- freshness
- current truth status
- evidence references
- provenance references
- governance notes
- engine version
- `supersedesId` when a newer finding replaces an older one

## Supersession rules
A newer finding may supersede an older one when:
- it covers the same scope
- it covers the same truth class
- it is based on fresher verified inputs
- it passes governance and minimum-threshold rules

Supersession should not delete history.
It should:
- preserve the older finding
- mark it `superseded`
- point the newer finding back to it

## Staleness rules
A finding becomes `stale` when:
- freshness window expires
- supporting evidence no longer updates
- stronger contrary evidence arrives but not enough to publish a replacement finding

Stale findings must not be treated as active truth in:
- admin dashboards
- partner feeds
- market signal outputs
- future Scout API surfaces

## Suppression rules
LISA must suppress findings when:
- minimum threshold is not met
- evidence is too weak
- scope is too narrow and risks identity leakage
- governance rules block publication

Suppressed findings may still exist internally for evaluation, but they are not publishable truth.

## Consumers
Approved consumers:
- admin observability
- market signals products
- partner dashboards
- future Scout API read surfaces

Disallowed direct consumers:
- raw client components importing LISA internals
- direct partner access to internal scoring chains
- any surface that would expose gated contact paths

## Runtime model
TradeScout should only consume LISA through a bounded runtime contract.

Supported modes:
- `tradescout_local`
- `json_file`
- `remote`

This allows the product to ship now without embedding full LISA internals.

## Relationship to Scout
Scout routes action.
LISA explains truth.

Scout decides:
- what the user should do next
- what route or system should handle the need

LISA determines:
- what the system knows now
- what changed
- what can be stated as governed truth

## Relationship to sellable data
LISA may use internal-only source material to produce externally sellable aggregate truth.

Allowed:
- aggregate market-state outputs
- activation-readiness findings
- county/category/surface intelligence

Not allowed:
- raw user-level data export
- household-level data sale
- contact or identity leakage

## First implementation target
Phase 1:
- live admin feed

Phase 2:
- stored findings table
- supersession logic
- stale/suppressed/current lifecycle

Phase 3:
- partner-safe outputs
- market signal tie-in
