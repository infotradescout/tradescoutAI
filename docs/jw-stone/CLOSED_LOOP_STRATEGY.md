**Branch home:** `jw-stone/strategy-closed-loop` (and included from `jw-stone/lane-isolation` index).

## Core strategy

Do not turn JW into a larger ordinary stone yard.

Build a **closed-loop stone supply network**:

**TradeScout captures what customers want → JW sources and verifies worldwide → inventory is imported, received, and made truthful → fabricators / architects / designers / builders / homeowners buy through their own paths → install happens through independent fabricators → HomeID preserves exact stone history → completed work creates more demand.**

Large distributors already win on warehouses, trucks, and commodity volume. Matching that burns cash. JW wins by:

1. Seeing demand earlier  
2. Finding unusual stone sooner (**JW sourcing**)  
3. Buying less bad inventory  
4. Selling new inventory faster (**sales execution, including Levon**)  
5. Serving all five customer types better  
6. Expanding through routes, partnerships, and acquisitions — not giant warehouses first  

---

## Part 1 — The real-world problem

1. **Backward inventory:** Guess → buy containers → warehouse → sell. Wrong guess traps cash.  
2. **Owner dependence:** Knowledge in people’s heads; ops unstable when key people leave.  
3. **One-size customer mistake:** Specifiers, schedulers, buyers, and end users need different paths — but **all five are customers**.  
4. **Durable demand:** Kitchen/bath remodeling and natural stone preference remain strong; professionals sit on much of the purchase path — serve professionals **and** homeowners without lying about inventory or prices.

**Win condition:** Control the path from inspiration → specification → purchase → delivery → install → permanent property record — not “own the most slabs.”

Market context references used in the owner brief (external): MSI / Triton scale, NKBA 2026 trends, Houzz renovation professional rates, NAHB remodeling outlook. Treat those as market backdrop, not TradeScout product claims.

## Part 2 — How it works

### 1. Structure the 85% acquisition correctly

Default: **new clean OpCo**

- Holding company: **85%**  
- Partner economic interest: **15%** (as negotiated — do not confuse with “Levon sources”)  
- OpCo acquires verified JW assets; assumes only listed debts  
- **TradeScout stays separate** and licenses platform/services to JW  

Assets must include name, customers, suppliers, phones, domains, social, media, contracts, deposits, open orders, vehicles, equipment, records, goodwill — not only slabs.

Prefer asset deal unless counsel/accountant prove stock deal benefits outweigh liability risk.

**Control must be real:** Florida LLC economic interest ≠ management rights. Manager-managed operating agreement must give majority side control of management, banking, records, budgets, employees, contracts, purchasing limits, and distributions.

Both-sides consent only for major items (sale of company, dissolve, unfair dilution, related-party abuse, change to 15% economics). Day-to-day ops stay under majority control.

**Price:** Inventory from verified landed cost, marked down for age/damage/mismatch/bad names/missing docs/slow demand. Goodwill partly via earnout on **cash-collected gross profit**. Seller note + holdback for hidden liabilities. Independent valuation, full inventory schedule, attorney-reviewed purchase agreement; IRS Form 8594 allocation as required. SBA 7(a) only if lender math works.

**People**

| Seat | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Majority / holding | Ownership, capital, expansion, TradeScout relationship, brand protection |
| Operating GM | Daily P&L, staff, sales execution, inventory, warehouse, service |
| **JW** | Chief sourcing / stone authority |
| **Levon** | Sales under GM discipline |
| Finance controller | Cash, AP/AR, landed cost, margins, inventory valuation |
| Purchasing gate | Approve buys using demand + margin + quality + cash (**JW recommends; gate approves money**) |

Majority control must not require the owner to become daily salesperson, dispatcher, or clerk.

### 2. Market in layers

1. Pensacola + practical delivery radius  
2. Eastern Gulf: Mobile, Fort Walton, Destin, Panama City, surrounds  
3. Gulf/Southeast via fixed routes, partner galleries, small cross-docks  
4. National **discovery** for rare material; **regional fulfillment** from real inventory  

**Control metrics (behavior):**

- Fabricators check JW before competitors  
- Architects/designers specify or start projects through JW  
- Builders put JW in standard/upgrade selections  
- Quarries/suppliers call **JW** first on rare material  
- Revenue from repeating professional accounts **and** clean homeowner-originated conversion  
- New material sells fast enough to free cash  

Website views ≠ market control.

### 3. Exact JW position

> **JW is the Gulf Coast’s first-look source for globally selected natural stone — sourced by JW’s expertise — backed by verified inventory, dependable delivery, and a trusted path for fabricators, architects, designers, builders, and homeowners.**

**Three inventory groups (opening management limits; reset after 90 days of real data)**

| Group | Opening mix | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | ~60% | Proven repeat buys; competitive but no blanket “beat any price”; match only like-for-like quotes |
| First Cut | ~25% | JW’s newest/strongest finds; trusted release — not fake exclusivity |
| Project lots | ~15% | Matched quantity/consistency/docs/schedule for builders and larger work |

**First Cut honesty**

- No retroactive “First Cut” filler  
- Inbound may be previewed; never shown as physically available  
- “Exclusive” only with written proof  
- If JW selected/named first, say that — do not overclaim  
- Placeholders never silently become inventory  

### 4. Capture demand before JW buys stone

JW never leaves with only “find beautiful material.”

**Private sourcing brief (TradeScout + JW ops) before each buy cycle:**

- Requested colors / warm–cool / movement language  
- Categories by fabricators, architects, designers, builders, homeowners  
- Wishlist activity; repeated unavailable requests  
- Lost quotes + reasons  
- Builder quantities; designer/architect samples  
- Geo demand; private accepted price bands  
- Do-not-duplicate vs replenish vs never-repeat slow stock  

**Purchase gate**

Expected collected sales  
− stone + origin + freight + duties + brokerage + port + inspection + damage + storage + local delivery + commissions + travel allocation + cost of cash  
= expected contribution  

Must survive slower sales. No container that only works if everything sells at top price immediately.

Each cycle: approved budget, categories, supplier caps, quality bar, origin photo/video, measurements/finish, defects, bundle match, landed cost estimate, **final approval before money commits**.

**Incentives**

- JW sourcing: sell-through and collected gross profit on material JW selected (e.g. 90–180 days) — not purchase volume  
- Levon / sales: collected gross profit, repeat accounts, payment quality — not invoice vanity  

### 5. Stone Passport

Start at stone/bundle level; claim live slab precision only when receiving + sales systems support it.

Record: verified name (or none), type, country/supplier, quarry if known, bundle/shipment ID, qty received, dimensions, thickness, finish as received, consistent photos, video, defects/repairs/resin/mesh/fissures, receive date, status, landed cost, location, sales history, customer interest, delivery/install when done.

**Not “available” until received, counted, inspected, photographed, verified.**  
Sold → quantity updates immediately. Unknown → say less.

**HomeID after install:** stone/name, supplier/bundle, fabricator/installer, date, finish, care, photos, warranty, future repair path.

### 6. Five customer paths (same company, different jobs)

#### Fabricators
Private accurate inventory; fast quotes; exact dims/finish/qty; route days; claims; matching photos; expiring project quotes; terms after payment history; first notice of relevant arrivals; real account manager; qualified homeowner connections by geo/performance (**never pay-to-rank**).  
JW stays **neutral** — no JW fabrication during market-capture.

#### Architects
Spec-ready docs; finish/application truth; matched lots; origin when verified; sample/control boards; schedule realism; handoff into builder/fabricator execution.

#### Designers
Private First Cut previews; samples; client boards; accurate sourcing stories (**JW-sourced**); care/application; matched material; arrival events; shareable project selections; clear handoff to client’s fabricator.

#### Builders
Repeatable core + marked upgrades; project lots; multi-home consistency; delivery planning; private project pricing; spec/closing docs; replacement path.

#### Homeowners
Large accurate photos; plain discovery (color/warmth/movement/room); persistent wishlist without account; **no public bait prices**; **no fake availability**; deliberate “ask about this stone”; path to verified local fabricator.

### 7. JW travel as commercial sourcing (not Levon theater)

**Before:** demand brief; countries/suppliers approved; budget; supplier scorecards; required materials; speculative cap; content requirements.  

**During:** verify supplier; inspect; dims/finish; defects; bundles together; replacement talk; freight/lead time; terms; photo/video tied to inbound SKUs; no side buys mixed with company money.  

**After:** review every proposed buy; update landed cost; attach to named demand; release audience-appropriate previews; score supplier after arrival.

Supplier goals: first look, first review of selected bundles, regional exclusivity where real, claims, naming/photo rights, prep standards, price protection, continuity for projects.

Contacts built on company funds belong to JW. No undisclosed side deals; company accounts only.

### 8. Logistics as a product

Price each shipment across feasible ports/routes. Pensacola is an option — not dogma.

**Fixed route days** first: Pensacola; Mobile; Fort Walton/Destin; Panama City; add only with account density.

Use insured carriers until ownership beats full cost per delivered slab.

Every move: A-frame ID, load photos, condition out, carrier confirm, delivery photos, custody, damage/claims, exact status.

### 9. Expand small before big warehouses

1. Sell from Pensacola  
2. Fixed delivery days  
3. Recurring accounts across all five customer types  
4. Samples/displays with real local partners  
5. Architect/designer/builder events  
6. Measure density + delivery cost  
7. Small appointment gallery / cross-dock only after proof  
8. Larger warehouse only when sales constrain space  

No fake locations or fake local inventory.

| Period | Objective | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before close | Real control | Clean count, financials, legal, liabilities, customer/supplier records |
| First 90 days | Accurate, owner-independent Pensacola | GM in, inventory verified, pricing controlled, customers contacted, daily scorecard |
| Months 4–12 | Dominate first delivery ring | Recurring accounts, profitable routes, ≥2 good sourcing cycles, positive operating cash |
| Year 2 | Route micro-markets | Recurring accounts + profitable density before leases |
| Years 3–5 | Southeast network | Central buying/inventory, repeatable branch, cash, acquisition capability |

No second full yard until yard one runs **90 days** without majority owner managing daily ops (JW may still advise sourcing under the purchase gate).

### 10. Acquisitions after the model works

Targets: strong local relationships, retiring owner, useful territory, weak sourcing/records/web/capital, clean liabilities, complementary base.

Centralize: global purchasing (**JW-led**), suppliers, finance, inventory truth, photo, private pricing, TradeScout discovery, marketing, logistics planning.  
Local: accounts, service, receiving, delivery, architect/designer/builder ties.

No roll-up until sell-through, GP, and retention prove out.

### 11. Team

GM; trade sales manager; fabricator reps; architect/designer/builder coverage; inventory/receiving; logistics; controller; sourcing coordinator **supporting JW**; photo/content; warehouse/delivery.

Pay for collected GP, repeat growth, payment quality, sell-through, claim rate, inventory accuracy — not volume theater or uncollected “sales.”

### 12. One scorecard (reset after 90 clean days)

| Control | Starting target |
| --- | --- |
| Inventory accuracy | 100% received material on verified record + photo + qty + finish + location |
| Quote response | Most normal trade quotes ≤30 min business hours |
| Delivery | ≥95% on time complete |
| New inventory | ≥70% sold in 90 days |
| Aged inventory | <10% of value >180 days |
| Repeat business | ≥60% monthly revenue from repeating accounts by end of year one |
| Concentration | No single customer >15% revenue |
| First Cut / JW buys | No meaningful buy without named demand, quality, landed cost, downside profit |
| New market | No permanent lease until routes profitable 3 months + recurring local accounts |
| Cash | Fixed-cost reserve + all approved inbound funded |
| Owner independence | Daily ops normal 90 days without either owner present |

**North-star number:** cash-collected gross profit per dollar tied in inventory.

## Part 3 — Safe and honest

- **Ownership:** 85% = control + banking + records, not paper distributions. Full schedules; Florida counsel + transaction accountant before close.  
- **Inventory truth:** no invented names, wrong categories, inbound-as-available, fake exclusive, wrong finish, unverified qty, sold-as-available, public bait pricing. Incomplete > false.  
- **Safety:** OSHA slab + silica reality; written unload/lift/rack/forklift/restraint/training/delivery/incident/carrier rules; never advertise natural stone as silica-free; fabricator partners verified for dust controls/insurance.  
- **Channel:** do not secretly compete with fabricator customers; TradeScout connections by area/verified work/outcomes — not highest bidder.  
- **Finance:** JW trips budgeted and gated; majority cash not unlimited speculation; distributions only after tax/debt/payroll/ops/inbound/reserve. TradeScout never inside JW’s creditor perimeter.

## Part 4 — Bottom line and sequence

**Best outcome:** not the fullest warehouse — the strongest path from global source to installed home.

- **JW** = trusted sourcing judgment  
- **Levon** = disciplined selling into the five customer bases  
- **TradeScout** = demand + connection  
- **Fabricators** stay independent and paid for real work  
- **HomeID** = permanent stone history  

**Takeover sequence**

1. Secure real 85% control (clean structure)  
2. Install GM before sourcing travel ramps  
3. Physical + financial inventory audit  
4. Split core / First Cut / project lots  
5. Build account networks across all five customer types  
6. Every buy cycle answers a recorded demand brief (**JW sources**)  
7. Stone Passport on every inbound  
8. Fixed regional delivery routes  
9. Dominate Pensacola + eastern Gulf  
10. Small footprints only after proof  
11. Acquire weak distributors after model works  
12. National First Cut demand + regional fulfillment discipline  

**Rule:**

> **Do not try to own the most stone. Own the strongest path from JW’s global source to the installed home — serving fabricators, architects, designers, builders, and homeowners as customers.**
