# TradeScout Everyday Guide

> This document governs all help articles, in-product guidance, and Scout explanations. It is the canonical source of truth for how TradeScout works.

This guide is written for everyday operators. Use it to answer three questions quickly:

- What does this page mean?
- What should I do next?
- Why is TradeScout asking me to follow this path?

## Connection Without Compromise

<!-- Stable anchor: #connection-without-compromise -->

TradeScout follows one rule across all surfaces: discovery is open, action is governed.

What this means:

- Seeing a profile, listing, or post does not automatically unlock contact.
- Scout is the bridge that turns discovery into guided action.
- Trust signals and local relevance decide exposure, not payment.
- The system blocks shortcuts that would bypass trust and authority.

When users ask "Why can't I just message directly?", the answer is:

- TradeScout protects quality and accountability by routing contact through intent and fit checks first.

## Direct Connect Workflow

<!-- Stable anchor: #direct-connect-workflow -->

Direct Connect is the operational path from intent to contact.

Sequence:

- Step 1: Define intent. User states what they need in plain language.
- Step 2: Route by fit. Scout and platform rules evaluate local relevance and trust signals.
- Step 3: Build decision context. User sees who fits and why.
- Step 4: Controlled contact. Contact opens only when workflow requirements are satisfied.

Operator rule:

- If a user is browsing but ready to act, route them into Direct Connect.

## Finances & Invoicing

<!-- Stable anchor: #finances-invoicing -->

Finances pages are for clarity, records, and execution confidence.

Core expectations:

- Every invoice should match a real decision or completed milestone.
- Status and record details should be explicit before closing an item.
- Financial flows should remain auditable and tied to local context.

When users are unsure what to do:

- Confirm status first (open, pending, complete).
- Resolve the oldest blocking record.
- Return to Scout for next-step routing if uncertainty remains.

## Messaging Rules

<!-- Stable anchor: #messaging-rules -->

Messaging is intentional, not an open blast channel.

Rules:

- Messaging supports active decision workflows.
- Threads should move toward a concrete next action.
- If context is missing, gather intent before expanding contact.

Why this exists:

- It prevents spam, protects trust, and improves response quality.

## Cancel & Reopen

<!-- Stable anchor: #cancel-reopen -->

Cancel and reopen flows should preserve clarity and accountability.

Cancel when:

- Scope changed materially.
- Timing is no longer valid.
- Trust or fit constraints are no longer satisfied.

Reopen when:

- User has updated context and a clear next objective.
- Required verification or records are now complete.

Operator standard:

- Document why it was canceled and what changed before reopening.

## Sharing & Attribution

<!-- Stable anchor: #sharing-attribution -->

Sharing should preserve origin context and trust continuity.

Principles:

- Shared links should keep local and decision context intact.
- Attribution should make source and intent understandable.
- Sharing should lead to a guided next step, not a dead end.

If sharing causes confusion:

- Route users back through Scout so intent and authority are re-established.

## FAQ Quick Answers

Use these answers as the default short response layer in Help surfaces.

- What is Scout?
	- Scout is TradeScout's guided bridge from discovery to action.
- Why is contact gated?
	- Gating protects trust, relevance, and accountability.
- Can payment change visibility?
	- No. Spend does not override trust/CVS rules.
- Where should users start when confused?
	- Start with Scout, then follow the recommended next action.
